#General
The algo:
- Each character has four XP thresholds by level (easy, medium, hard, deadly). The sum of these across the party = party thresholds.
- Each monster has an XP value from its CR.
- Sum raw monster XP.
- Multiply by a factor based on monster count because six goblins are harder than one suped up goblin worth six times the XP.
- Compare adjusted XP against the party thresholds to make sure the fight is fair (or not >:V ).
- exp logic uses values because the exp will never change outside of the rules I set. Combat logic uses entities because individual players and monsters will have unique states

#models.py
Why frozen=True?
  These are values, not entities. Two level 5 characters are interchangeable for XP purposes. Immutability means I'll never accidentally mutate one halfway through a calculation and spend an hour wondering why the numbers drift.
Why validate in __post_init__?
  Invalid states should be unrepresentable. If a PartyMember exists, its level is valid and every downstream function can stop checking.

#encounter.py
rate_encounter returns the adjusted xp alongside the difficulty because I plan to show how close an encounter is to the next tier when I get to making the UI. Having a visual like a progess bar will be more useful to beginner DMs than simply telling them what tier the encounter is at currently. An encounter that is just barely deadly and one that is an almost guaranteed TPK should be easily distinguished visually.

I also implemented clamping in encounter_multiplier. Since the DMG doesn't specify what to do with edge case combat encounters that go past the ends of the table, we're just gonna treat them as the nearest difficulty tier.

#combat.py
advance_turn is the only fucntion that impacts turn progression so I'm routing round increments, condition decrements, and general turn order through it. I want to avoid various counters and timers being slightly off depending on the function that calls them and causing bugs
CombatError will return something specific for the FastAPI layer to catch and map to a 409
Overflow check happens before clamping, instant death from a massive hit campares the excess damage against max hp so I need the negative value before I zero it out
Concentration is dropped upon falling unconscious so the player won't roll to maintain concentration and no check is issued
The concentration dc uses the damage to hp and not total hp. Apparently the actual rules uses the total damage of the hit even if some is mitigated. I think that's stupid so I won't be doing that.
Decided to make the damage reporting a lil bit more complex cuz the damage is being applied properly with mitigation from temp_hp calculated properly but for the sake of the UI I'd like more info so I can, if I want, visually display why a hit is doing less damage than it should ex:20 damage (7 absorbed)
